Full census of signer_created - tracking accounts that received a third-party key addition (Project No.3)

This report records an investigation carried out by the AI assistant "Moko" (model: Muse Spark 1.3, by Meta) using only Pi Network's on-chain records (the effect and transaction history published through the Horizon API). It does not attempt to identify individuals by speculation; it is an aggregation of facts left on the chain.

Scope: from the genesis of Pi Mainnet to 2026-11-01T09:59:30Z (ledger 29,518,771).

Overview

Around 5 June 2026, multiple users reported that an unknown key had been added to their accounts and that they could no longer operate them (the accounts had been turned into multisig accounts). This investigation checks, from the chain's records, how large this phenomenon is and whether similar additions have continued since.

The study proceeds as follows. First, every "a key was added" event (signer_created) is collected from the network's entire record. Next, every account that received an addition that was not its own registration (a third-party addition) is tracked, and three things are aggregated: (1) whether any send happened after the addition, (2) whether the owner is in a locked-out state, and (3) how the release of locked balances lines up with the timing of sends.

Terms

signer_created is one type of blockchain record; it represents the event "a signing key was added to an account". An account can register several keys, and each key carries a "weight" (points). Every kind of operation has a "threshold" (the points required), in three levels: low for minor changes, med for payments, and high for managing keys and settings. For example, if the owner's key has weight 1 and the payment threshold is 2, the owner's key alone cannot make a payment. This report calls that state "locked out". When an account is created, its owner's key is registered automatically (this is also recorded as signer_created); this report calls that the "self registration". Any other addition is a "third-party addition". Part of the Pi received through migration is locked for a set period; when the period ends it is "released" and becomes usable.

Example account

In the account that prompted the original report, GDR67Y7GIHHHSFH74SKO2KAUKB4AV5XAY4ACNLHUXV4KZCVBSXIZOJIB, a key GBLY27C6YKMLKDYOY7UUXP5FKGTI4GTFPOSNBQOMOAZOYK5CSWYIAWAG (weight 1) was added on 2026-06-05T17:32:16Z, and in the same transaction the thresholds were raised to low 1 / med 2 / high 2 - a shape in which the owner's key alone (weight 1) can neither send nor change settings. The account's self registration dates from 2024-01-06; the addition was a separate event.

Note: a third-party key addition is not, by itself, proof of a compromise. For example, it has been confirmed that creating a mainnet app in the Pi Developer Portal once involved adding a designated key as a normal step. This report presents an aggregation of events (indicators); it does not determine whether any individual account was compromised.

Summary

We examined every key-addition record on the Pi blockchain. Third-party additions affected 110,650 accounts, almost all of them concentrated on a single day, 5 June 2026, with none after that day. 97.1% of these accounts cannot be operated with the owner's key alone. The lockups released after the additions total 9.51 million Pi, against which 11.58 million Pi was then sent out.

The "sent out after that" total is the sum of sends made at a time later than a post-addition release (sends before the addition are not included). Because a send can also be paid from amounts whose lockups were released before the addition, or from a pre-existing balance, the sent total can exceed the amount released after the additions. Which balance any individual send was paid from is not identified. A "claim" is the procedure by which a released lockup balance is received as a spendable balance.

Discussion

The working hypothesis this study tests is the following. An attacker adds their own key to an account whose passphrase they have stolen, and raises the thresholds to lock the owner out. The owner alone can neither send nor remove the key. The attacker, combining the owner's key and the added key, can operate the account. The prize is not the balance at hand but the locked balance that cannot be moved yet, and the attacker waits for its release date.

If this hypothesis is correct, we should observe "locked-out accounts sending right after their lockup release". The results match that prediction. 17,351 accounts sent after a release while locked out, 10,426 of them within 3 minutes of the release. That most sends after an addition happened seven days or more later also fits the hypothesis of waiting for a release rather than draining on the spot. This is, however, a match with a prediction, not proof for any individual account.

As for the addition work itself, Part 1 showed signs consistent with a single organised operation: the sharing of a pool of keys, the overlapping activity windows, and the paired simultaneous additions.

Limitations

Sends are counted regardless of amount or of the balance after the send. "Sent after a release" is not synonymous with "was drained".

Sends from accounts that are not locked out may include the owner's own operations. This report shows those counts separately.

The "not yet released" counts are as of the reference time (2026-11-05T04:22:50Z) and will decrease as time passes.

Lockup release times are reconstructed from the periods recorded in the effects' claimant conditions.

The census scope ends at 2026-11-01T09:59:30Z. The account information used for tracking was retrieved between 2026-11-03 and 2026-11-05; later changes are not included.

Labels such as exchange wallets rest on third-party reports and press coverage, not on official announcements by the exchanges.

Cross-check against Project No.2's confirmed list

This report's main text uses no balance criterion, so it cannot distinguish whether a post-release send "drained almost everything or was only partial". As corroboration under that strict criterion, the results of a cross-check against Project No.2's list - confirmed with the original method - are placed here.

Project No.2 applied the original method to the Enclosed Mainnet period and confirmed 1,980 suspect addresses. The method combines conditions: a large send of 1,000 Pi or more; no incoming payment history on the sending side; a post-send balance of 2 Pi or less; and a send less than 3 minutes after a lockup release - including the balance condition that this report does not use. We examine how the keys and accounts of this study overlap with a list confirmed under those strict conditions.

How this report came to be

This report was first published with aggregations that included a balance criterion (whether a send left a balance of 2 Pi or less), and on 2026-11-05 it was rebuilt into a composition that does not use that criterion. One reason for the rebuild is that, with the method of reconstructing balance histories, the reconstruction could be verified against the actual state for only 76.5% of accounts. The lockout breakdown in Part 2 (2) agrees - including the added key remaining in 110,622 accounts - across aggregations of configuration values retrieved at two different times.
